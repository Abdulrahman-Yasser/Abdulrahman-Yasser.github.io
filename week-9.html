From Checkerboard to ChArUco: Real Intrinsics and Independent Camera Parameters

Month DD – Month DD, 2026 Abdulrahman

This week

  • Properly calibrate the cameras to get real intrinsics instead of the scaled estimate (fx = fy = 2714.0 * (img_w / 3280.0)) used for K this week
  • Record two videos in full sunlight for better lighting, with three large checkerboards laid on the ground, to finish validating the BEV stitching
  • Start working on the multi-blending algorithm
  • Gave each of the 4 cameras its own independent height/pitch/yaw parameters

Details

Context

Last week's K was a placeholder — a single scaled estimate (fx = fy = 2714.0 * (img_w / 3280.0)) standing in for real intrinsics. This week was about replacing that guess with an actual calibration, and it turned into more back-and-forth than expected.

Standard checkerboard, intrinsic calibration

Started with the normal approach: a regular checkerboard held up in front of each camera from several angles to solve for the intrinsic calibration (focal length, principal point, distortion coefficients). That part went fine — got real numbers out instead of the scaled estimate.

The shared ground-plane board didn't work

Next step was the shared ground plane between the two cameras, which is what the BEV homography is built on. For that I printed a much bigger checkerboard and laid it on the ground where both cameras' fields of view overlap. The problem: it has to be fully visible in both cameras at once for the corner detection to work, and with the cameras' actual mounting positions and FOV, that just wasn't happening — one camera would always clip part of the board.

Switching to a ChArUco board

That's what led me to the ChArUco board — a checkerboard with ArUco markers embedded in the squares. Since each square is individually identifiable by its marker, the board doesn't need to be fully visible to be usable; partial views still give enough correspondences to solve the geometry. While reading up on it I also saw it's generally more accurate for intrinsic calibration than a plain checkerboard, since ArUco IDs remove corner-ordering ambiguity and the detector is more robust to partial occlusion and angle. So I went back and redid the intrinsic calibration with the ChArUco board too, not just the ground-plane part.

Results

The ChArUco runs used fewer views (27 and 18, vs. 48/43/33/24) and still landed a noticeably lower RMS — 0.43 px and 0.41 px, both comfortably under the "good" threshold, versus the 0.53–0.90 px spread from the plain checkerboard batches.

Wayland preview: up to 4 videos

Also extended last week's Wayland preview code, which only handled two cameras, to accept up to 4 videos at once. Each video now has its own independently editable position/height/pitch/yaw/pixel_per_m and the rest of the live-tunable parameters, instead of one shared set for the whole preview.
